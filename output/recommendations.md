## Rules

- **Assortment: Add: missing subcategory × shade group.** A subcategory × shade group where competitors are hot (mean competitor signal score ≥ 50 and mean competitor trend ≥ +15% (top-right of the competitor view)) and we list no product. Estimate: min–max 12-week revenue per active product of the nearest three represented groups by |score difference|/100 + |trend difference|, × products suggested.
- **Assortment: Expand: thinly covered group.** A hot group (same definition) where we list products but only 1 to 2 are active and in stock. Estimate: min–max 12-week revenue per active product of the same three nearest peer groups × products suggested.
- **Assortment: Shelf space needed.** Products suggested = number of hot competitor products in the group (at least 1; 1 for a relist). Shelf needed = products suggested × median shelf space of our active products in the same subcategory (overall median when none are active there); a relist uses the product's own shelf space.
- **Assortment: Relist product.** An inactive product that sells under 1% of its market, in a market of at least €25,000 growing +20% or more.
- **Assortment: Under-performing group.** A subcategory × shade group whose share of market revenue is below 60% of the category share (6.3%), in a market of at least €25,000.
- **Products: Quadrant decision rules.** Market unit demand splits at the median; fair-share index (channel unit share ÷ market unit share) splits at 1. Active, available bottom-right products are under-sold (push); active bottom-left products are low performers (review viability).
- **Products: Push: high vs medium.** High: market of at least €100,000, market trend +20% or more and fair-share index 0.75 or lower. All other under-sold products are medium.
- **Products: Low performer: high vs medium.** High: revenue in the bottom quartile (up to €1,306) with both channel and market trend −15% or worse. All other low performers are medium.
- **Products: Low performer coverage.** Counts the other active, not out-of-stock products in the same subcategory × shade group. When none remain, removing the product empties the group: keep or replace it rather than delist.
- **Price: Price outlier.** Price index = our price ÷ market average selling price (market revenue ÷ market units). Below market at 0.90 or less, above market at 1.20 or more. Large gap marks indices at 0.80 or less / 1.40 or more (magnitude, not confidence). The market average is a realized proxy, not a shelf price.
- **Price: Elasticity scenario.** Target price = market average selling price. Units at target = units × (target ÷ price)^elasticity, with assumed elasticities -1 and -2 (assumptions: no elasticity was measured). Unit cost proxy = price × (1 − reported margin). Gross-profit uplift = units at target × (target − cost) − units × (price − cost); revenue change shown alongside. Only positive uplift counts; inactive products count zero.
- **Availability: Restock.** An active product that is out of stock, or on low stock in a market growing +20% or more.

## Estimate bases

- Q1: Missing and thinly covered subcategory × shade groups: min–max 12-week revenue per active product in the three nearest represented groups (by competitor score and trend) × products suggested (sparse analogues, not forecasts). Relist products: low reaches the 25th-percentile product share (3.8%) of their market, high the median product share (5.1%). Under-performing groups (high case only): lift to 3.8% share (60% of category share 6.3%), excluding products counted in other recommendations. A group that is both thinly covered and under-performing is counted once (the larger estimate).
- Q2: Low: under-sold products reach the 25th-percentile product share (3.8%) of their market, or keep 50% of current revenue where the channel trails the market by 30+ points (the larger). High: median product share (5.1%), or 100% of current revenue where sales are collapsing (the larger). Only products below these targets receive a positive estimate; low performers are not monetised.
- Q3: Move each active outlier (price index ≤ 0.90 or ≥ 1.20) to the market average selling price. Units at target = units × (target ÷ price)^elasticity, with assumed elasticities -1 and -2 (assumptions, not measured). Unit cost proxy = price × (1 − reported margin); gross-profit uplift = units at target × (target − cost) − units × (price − cost). Range = smaller and larger positive uplift of the two elasticities; negative uplifts count as zero. Validate costs, promotions and elasticity before acting.
- Q4: Low: 50% of the 12-week revenue of 12 active out-of-stock products. High: 100% of it plus 25% of the 12-week revenue of 11 low-stock products in markets growing +20% or more.
- General: 12-week estimates, not forecasts. Individual question ranges overlap. The combined revenue total takes the largest revenue estimate per product across actions, plus group-level estimates (missing, thinly covered and under-performing subcategory × shade groups, the larger one per group). The Q3 price estimate is gross profit and is reported separately, not added.

## Caveats

- Stock status is a snapshot: there is no on-hand quantity, lead time or lost-sales data.
- Competitor signals are one snapshot (2026-07-01) from mixed sources (retailer rank, search, social); read them as direction, not volume.
- Competitor pack sizes are unknown, so competitor price comparisons are indicative.
- Price elasticities (-1 and -2) are assumptions; the price estimates show what happens if demand reacts that way, not what will happen.
- The data is synthetic: the strongest signals sit in a handful of products, so conclusions rest on few items.
- Channel and market 'share' is a ratio of channel revenue to a market-demand proxy, not a true market share.
- Question ranges overlap. The separately calculated combined total takes the largest estimate per product; peer-group estimates assume no cannibalization and may share a small peer sample.
