# Hair Coloration: category opportunity review

Zenline FDE take-home. A small Python script turns the SKU, product and competitor data for ZenBeauty Retail (Beauty > Hair Coloration) into an interactive, multi-page category review. There is one page per business question, and each recommendation carries a size-of-prize range.

## Open or regenerate

- **Open:** `output/index.html` in a browser. It works straight from disk with no server, no internet and no install.
- **Plain text:** `output/recommendations.md` has the recommendations and every list. `output/actions.csv` has one row per flagged SKU or cell, for Excel.
- **Regenerate:** run `python3 review.py`. It needs Python 3.9+ and the standard library only.

## Pages

| Page | Question | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| Recommendations (`index.html`) | – | See the four recommendations, sorted by size of prize. Click one to open its analysis page. |
| 1 Assortment | Do you have the right assortment? | Compare competitor signals with our own performance per subcategory × shade group. The two charts are linked. Drill into products and see gap and under-performing cells. |
| 2 Products | Are you selling the right products? | Compare market demand with our fair-share index, by SKU, cell or brand. See the "not pushed enough" and "low performing" lists. |
| 3 Price | Are you selling at the right price? | See exact competitor matches, price position and a quadrant of price index vs trend gap. The price-review list is tiered Confirmed or Flag. |
| 4 Availability | Are the products available? | See stock status by supplier, a "restock first" quadrant, a supplier scorecard and the restock list. |
| Method | – | See the thresholds, how products are flagged, prize bases, how to read the charts, data questions and caveats. |

Every chart has quadrant names, tooltips with the underlying numbers, click-to-drill with a back button, and a table under it that follows the current selection.

## Recommendations

All figures are per 12 weeks and generated by `review.py`. The index page sorts them by the high end of the range (`REC_SORT` in `review.py` switches this to question order).

| Q | Action | Key evidence | Size of prize (illustrative) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | Restock 12 out-of-stock SKUs and 11 low-stock SKUs in rising markets; start with SalonPro Distribution | The 12 active out-of-stock SKUs carry €116,181 of revenue. SalonPro Distribution accounts for 33% of the revenue on low or out-of-stock SKUs. | €58,090–€138,796 revenue protected |
| 3 | Review price on 2 confirmed SKUs and check 34 more flagged SKUs | HC0006 sells at €4.77, 40% below the identical product at rossmann.de (€7.95). | €17,233–€34,467 gross margin |
| 2 | Push 5 under-sold SKUs in growing markets and review 14 low performers | HC0001 has a €181,000 market growing +18%, but its channel trend is −99%. | €14,895–€24,928 revenue |
| 1 | Relist 2 delisted high-demand SKUs and close 11 assortment gaps; lift 12 under-performing cells | HC0007 and HC0003 have €0 channel sales in markets worth €382,500 growing at least +27%. | €14,377–€22,592 revenue |

## How the prize ranges are sized

These are illustrative scenarios, not forecasts. They overlap partly, so do not add them together.

- **Q1:**
  - Low: the relisted SKUs reach the 25th-percentile share of their market among active SKUs (3.8%).
  - High: they reach the median share (5.1%), and under-performing cells rise to 60% of the category share. SKUs already counted in another recommendation are left out.
- **Q2:**
  - Low: each under-sold SKU reaches the 25th-percentile share, or keeps 50% of its revenue if its sales are collapsing, whichever is larger.
  - High: the median share, or 100% of its revenue.
- **Q3 (gross margin):** 50% (low) or 100% (high) of the margin at stake. Margin at stake = (competitor price − our price) × units for identical products priced 15%+ below a competitor, plus the gap to the median margin × revenue for thin-margin top sellers.
- **Q4:**
  - Low: 50% of the 12-week revenue of active out-of-stock SKUs.
  - High: 100% of it, plus 25% of the revenue of low-stock SKUs in markets growing 20% or more.

## How it works

`review.py` loads the three CSVs and joins them on `product_id`. It then:

1. Computes the metrics, the cell, brand and supplier rollups, the lists and the prize ranges.
2. Copies the hand-written site from `web/` into `output/`.
3. Writes all the numbers to `output/assets/data.js`.

The pages are plain HTML. One shared chart component (`web/assets/charts.js`) draws every quadrant chart in SVG. All thresholds sit in one dictionary, `T`, at the top of `review.py`, and the Method page lists them.

## Assumptions

- `channel_*` is the retailer's own sales and `market_*` is a demand proxy for the same product. "Share" means channel ÷ market, a ratio to a proxy, not a measured market share.
- Trend fields are fractional change over 12 weeks (0.18 = +18%).
- `price_eur` is the current shelf price. Revenue often differs from price × units (promotions).
- In the competitor data, a lower rank is better and a higher signal score is better. Popularity uses the score, which is comparable across sources.
- Stock status is a snapshot: there is no on-hand quantity, lead time or lost-sales data.

## Known limits

- The data is synthetic. Most SKUs track their market closely, and the strong signals sit in a handful of SKUs (HC0001–HC0007).
- Competitor rows are sparse: most cells have 0–1 competitor products. Only 2 rows match a client SKU exactly, and competitor pack sizes are unknown. For that reason, price vs the competitor cell median is shown as context but is not counted as a signal.
- The market-price signal fires on 24 SKUs, which keeps the Flag tier of the price list long (34 items). Treat Flag as "look at", not "act on".
- `inaktiv` SKUs still sold €191k. Their meaning is an open data question, so they are shown separately rather than excluded.
- Thresholds were set by looking at this data once. They are not tuned or back-tested, and there are no automated tests.

## Next improvements

1. Add weekly sales and stock on hand, so lost sales can be estimated rather than using "revenue exposed".
2. Match competitor products to SKUs by EAN, or by a reviewed fuzzy match, to widen the price comparison.
3. Let the category manager accept, reject or annotate each item and feed that back into the thresholds.
4. Add tests for the rules and input validation for the CSVs.
